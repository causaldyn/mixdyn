# The M4 run: pre-registration

**Frozen on 2026-11-21 on the author's confirmation, before any confirmatory world of the run is
fitted.** Every slot is filled. Round 8, the research round that read the tested rung's candidate
rule once on fresh worlds, has ended: its result and the scope chosen after it are below
(Deviations, item 11). This file, `m4.json` beside it and the sizing script it names,
`m4_sizing.py`, are the M4 run's pre-registration from the commit that marks them frozen. A change
after that commit is a deviation: the result names it and says why.

M3's verdicts stand as published and are not read again here. M3's scored worlds are M4's sizing
records. M4's claim is read on confirmatory worlds no run has read: each confirmatory block past the
worlds M3 read.

## Question

On each family of known-truth worlds that M4 covers, and at each of its rungs, does the engine plan
the quarter with a regret per euro of budget no worse than each maintained tool's, and no worse
than the status quo's and the equal split's, by more than a margin δ?

M4 confirms on fresh worlds what M3 left exploratory: families 1, 4, 6 and 14, which M3 read on M2's
scored worlds, and families 12 and 13 at the rungs M3's sizing cap left out. Round 8 froze a rule for
the tested rung, and M4 reads both rungs of every national family it covers, the tested rung with
that rule.

## The claim

- **Axis.** Regret per euro of the quarter's budget, as the bench scores it. Lower is better.
- **A comparison** is the claim's arm less one rival, at one rung of one family, paired by world,
  on the complete cases: the worlds where both the claim's arm and the rival made a plan, each arm's
  failures counted beside it. An environment where the rival failed on more than half of the worlds
  is not compared, and a rival compared in no environment leaves the claim. The reading with the
  status quo standing in for every failed plan is printed beside it as a secondary readout. It is
  within the margin where Hall's (1992) one-look reader shows the mean below δ at one-sided 0.025
  (`causaldyn_bench.scorecard.gate`, `Gate(reader="hall")`). Its printed word, `beats`, `ties` or
  `loses to`, is Hall's reader's too.
- **δ = 0.05 per euro.**
- **A verdict** is one family at one rung: an intersection-union test (Berger 1982) over the
  comparisons it claims, read at the largest of their levels.
- **A fixed sequence.** The verdicts are tested one at a time at one-sided 0.025, in the declared
  order (`Declaration.order`, ADR 0081): (0, 0), (0, 4), (2, 0), (2, 4), (3, 0), (3, 4), (5, 0),
  (5, 4), (7, 0), (7, 1), (12, 0), (12, 4), (17, 0), (17, 4), (18, 4), (20, 4), (18, 0), (19, 4),
  (13, 0), (20, 0), (19, 0), (14, 4), (4, 4), (6, 4), (13, 4), (1, 4). It holds each family and
  rung that claims a comparison and is not exploratory (Sizing), by its predictive on the sizing
  records (every claimed comparison within together), highest first; ties by family, then rung;
  family 7's tested rung named 1. A verdict holds where it is shown at 0.025 and every verdict
  before it held. The first verdict that is not shown ends the confirmatory claims: every later
  verdict is read and printed `not confirmed: the sequence stopped at family F rung R`. The sequence
  holds the familywise error at 0.025 with no adjustment (Maurer, Hothorn and Lehmacher 1995). The
  order names only verdicts the claim reads (`Declaration.reads`).

- **A comparison is claimed** only where its predictive (Sizing) is at least 0.5. Every other
  comparison is run, read and printed, `not claimed: predicted to fail`, and listed below before
  the freeze. A family and rung whose every comparison is predicted to fail is printed `not claimed`.
- **The headline**, "within δ of every maintained MMM", is against each rival at its recommended
  settings. It is said only where every family and rung is claimed whole, no comparison predicted
  to fail, and every verdict holds: an intersection-union test over the verdicts, each at 0.025,
  with no adjustment. It cannot be said: the k = 0 rungs of families 1, 4, 6 and 14 are past the
  cap, and family 4's has two comparisons predicted to fail (Predicted failures).
- **The losses.** Each comparison is read the other way too, the rival less the claim's arm
  against 0; Holm's step-down over the claim's comparisons is printed beside the verdicts.
- **The no-test clause.** At k = 0, on every family, the claim's arm less the status quo has a
  paired interval not above 0, read on every family and reported beside the claim.

## Sizing

`m4_sizing.py` beside this file (SHA-256
`9d0069c8d5790d3628f50a341b598b2a5e5561356cad1bfae7deef22bf68a9e7`), M3's rule and the tested
rung's rule below, on the sizing records (above):

1. **N.** For each family and rung, the fewest worlds, in tens, that give 80 % power at a true
   tie: N = (2.8016 · sd / δ)², sd the largest paired SD (ddof 1) of the verdict's comparisons with
   the recommended rivals, every complete case, the family's environments pooled. N is sized at
   0.025 a verdict: the fixed sequence tests each verdict at 0.025. A family declares the largest N
   over its rungs, at least the floor of 240, split evenly over its environments and rounded up to
   whole worlds an environment. A family and rung whose N passes the cap of 520 is exploratory:
   run, read and printed, never in the fixed sequence; a family whose largest N passes the cap
   declares the floor. This is M3's rule unchanged. Here it sizes k = 0; rule 2 sizes the tested
   rung and the family.
2. **The tested rung** (Deviations, item 12). Its worlds are the larger of round 8's recommendation
   and the N of rule 1 on `anchored floored`, at most the cap of 520, and every tested rung is
   claimed. Round 8 recommends 520 on families 1 and 13, 360 on family 4 and 240 on the rest: the
   worlds a verdict needs for 80 % power with the margin through the `anchored held` bridge at its
   one-sided 80 % upper bound (420, 340 and 240), raised on families 1 and 13 for the risk of having
   selected the rule. A k = 0 rung past the cap stays exploratory and does not size its family. A
   family declares the largest of its claimed rungs' worlds, at least the floor of 240, split evenly
   over its environments and rounded up to whole worlds an environment.
3. **The predictive** (`scorecard.development.predictive`), on the sizing records: the two-stage
   bootstrap M3 ordered its sequence by. In each environment the records' worlds are drawn with
   replacement, as many as it holds, and from those the declared worlds an environment; each draw
   is read as the claim reads it, each comparison on its complete cases, by Hall's reader against
   δ. A comparison's predictive is the share of draws that read it within; a verdict's is the share
   that read every claimed comparison within together. 4 000 resamples, seed 20261120.
4. **The sizes taken**: rules 1 and 2, decided on 2026-11-20 with scope 2. The draft's other
   options are not taken: M3's rule at 90 % power, every family at the cap of 520, and N at M3's
   observed mean difference.

**What the tie rule does here.** The rule sizes for a true tie. On families 1, 4, 6 and 14 the
comparison that binds is one the claim's arm wins by far on M3's records: Orbit at k = 0 (SD 0.41 to
0.69, mean -0.18 to -0.37 on families 1, 6 and 14), and the equal split at the tested rung of
families 4 and 14 (SD 0.48 and 0.77, mean -0.48 and -0.59). So the k = 0 rungs of families 1, 4, 6
and 14 pass the cap at 80 %, though most of those comparisons' predictives are 0.999 or more. The
tested rung's comparisons that can fail are those near a tie, which the rule does not see:
PyMC-Marketing on families 1 (+0.012), 6 (+0.020) and 14 (-0.006), Orbit on family 4 (-0.001) and
the equal split on family 13 (+0.005); their predictives at the declared worlds are 0.894, 0.943,
0.969, 0.961 and 0.901. On family 4 at k = 0, `margin held` less PyMC-Marketing is +0.184 and less
Orbit +0.171: past δ at any N.

## Records reused

None. Every rival is fitted on every M4 world; no record of another run is taken in place of a fit,
and no root declares a reuse.

## Reading

- **No interim look.** Every declared world of a family is fitted and scored before its verdicts
  are read.
- **A failed fit** is a failure record after two attempts. It is counted, and it leaves
  the complete cases of every comparison that reads it.
- **A plan off the box or the budget** is moved into the box and counted.
- **A fit past its chains' bounds** at any stage, the two-stage arm's first stage included, an
  R-hat above 1.01 or divergent draws past their share (`scorecard.report.past`): the claim reads
  the plan as made. A secondary reading gives every such plan the status quo's regret.

## Reported regardless

- Every verdict and every comparison's mean, interval, level and word, the predicted failures
  among them; Holm's losses beside them.
- **Secondary readings, never read by the claim** (`scorecard.claim.secondary_lines`): the
  comparisons with every fit past its bounds at the status quo's regret; the comparisons at
  δ = 0.01; at k = 0 of each family, the gain the worlds hold, the best plan less the status quo,
  and Meridian's plan less the status quo.
- The status-quo-fallback readout, each arm's failures, the exploratory families and rungs, the
  no-test clause on every family, and every recorded arm less every rival (`claim read --recorded`).
- Cost: each process's CPU-seconds, wall and peak memory (`logs/costs.jsonl`), and each attempt
  (`logs/attempts.jsonl`).
- **Family 15**, not run: round 8's development reading of its rule on family 15's 100 recorded
  worlds, beside the claim and never in it. The rule less Meridian +0.033 (at its one-sided 80 %
  upper bound +0.037), less Orbit +0.033 (+0.036), less PyMC-Marketing +0.029 (+0.031);
  `anchored held` less Meridian +0.008. On family 15 the rule costs +0.025 against
  `anchored held`.

## Stop rules

- **No stop on an outcome.** Neither a win nor a loss stops a family early.
- **A world's limits.** Each arm runs under its memory cap and timeout (`Declaration.limits`) by
  M3's rule: M2's for the arm's cost class on the family its cost is read from (families 12, 13 and
  17 to 20 family 0's), the timeout stretched by the arm's cost factor; each family's in `m4.json`'s
  `limits` at the freeze. One CPU a process, but Meridian 1.2 and PyMC-Marketing 1.5. A world
  stopped twice is a failure record.
- **A defect found during the run**: that arm stops, the fix is committed, and every world of that
  arm is fitted again; the result names it as a deviation.
- **The budget**: $51.61 in all, the worst case of one c6a.48xlarge spot box (Compute, below),
  approved by the author on 2026-11-21 and fixed before the run starts: the AWS credits left after
  M3 (not read at the freeze) pay first, the author the rest. The cost model puts the stage at
  1 868 core-hours, $27.0 at the median and $35.2 at the 90th percentile. A guard keeps the worst
  case under the budget (Compute, below). If it runs out, every family and rung whose declared
  worlds are whole is read; the others are reported as not run.

## Compute

From the run kit's cost model on the declared worlds, M2's recorded CPU a process, one simulation on
one 192-CPU box: a planning figure and not a timing. Prices an hour, spot: m6a.48xlarge 1.41 at the
median (what M3 paid) and 1.78 at the 90th percentile; c6a.48xlarge 1.22 and 1.34. Box hours are the
makespan times 1.075 for spot stops, with half an hour to provision and half an hour to pull.

Scope 2, `anchored floored` not routed, the sizes taken; 51 280 processes. Core-hours by the model's

an hour, else not launched. The m6a.48xlarge row is priced and not taken: its worst case, $62.82, is
past the budget. Both are third-generation AMD EPYC, the class M3's box was, with 192 CPUs. M3 did
not use a c6a.48xlarge, which has half the m6a's memory: the model put M3's stage there at 77 box
hours at the median. Without `dlm`, Robyn and `meridian tuned` the model puts M4's stage on it
within 1.1 hours of the m6a's makespan, its largest reservation 343 of its 369 GiB.

**The guard.** The budget holds in the worst case, not at the 90th percentile, as in M3. The box
keeps a clock of its billed hours, its uptime summed over its boots (`m4-autostop.sh`). After the
deadline no world starts whose 90th-percentile wall would end past it (`M4_DEADLINE_HOURS`, which
`run-box.sh` turns into `M4_DEADLINE` at each start); at the hard stop the box powers itself off,
once it has also been up 45 minutes since its last boot. Neither reads an outcome. The deadline is
the 90th-percentile box hours plus one, rounded up to a half hour; the hard stop is three hours
past it; the spot bid (`MaxPrice`) is the 90th-percentile price plus 2 %. The worst case is the
hard stop plus an hour at the bid with the address, a 200 GiB gp3 volume for 5 days, the image's
snapshot for a month and 30 GB out, the volume at $0.12 a GiB-month as in M3. On a c6a: the
deadline at 27.0 billed hours, the hard stop at 30.0 (108 000 s), the bid at most $1.37 an hour, the
worst case $51.61. On an m6a: 25.5, 28.5 (102 600 s), $1.82, $62.82. The box's guard takes the row
of the type launched. Against the budget: the type launched is the c6a.48xlarge, and its worst
case, $51.61, is the budget (Stop rules).

If the deadline binds, the roots with the most work are cut first: every root advances in seed
order. A family and rung whose declared worlds are not whole is reported as not run, and the fixed
sequence stops at its first such verdict.
