# The M5 run: pre-registration

**Frozen on 2026-11-23 on the decision the author delegated, before any confirmatory world of the
run is fitted.** Every slot is filled. This file, `m5.json` beside it and the sizing script it
names, `m5_sizing.py`, are the M5 run's pre-registration from the commit that marks them frozen. A
change after that commit is a deviation: the result names it and says why.

M3's and M4's verdicts stand as published and are not read again here. M5 reads only what M4 left
open, on confirmatory worlds no run has read: a third confirmatory run, each environment's worlds
from a block of its own.

## Question

On each family of known-truth worlds that M5 covers, and at each of its rungs, does the engine plan
the quarter with a regret per euro of budget no worse than each maintained tool's, and no worse
than the status quo's and the equal split's, by more than a margin δ?

M4 confirmed 26 of 26 verdicts. Its tie rule left the k = 0 rungs of families 1, 6 and 14 past the
cap, so M4 read them only as exploratory, and M4 did not run family 15, whose worlds are files. M5
reads those four families with M4's arms, unchanged.

## The claim

- **Axis.** Regret per euro of the quarter's budget, as the bench scores it. Lower is better.
- **A comparison** is the claim's arm less one rival, at one rung of one family, paired by world,
  on the complete cases, as in M4: an environment where the rival failed on more than half of the
  worlds is not compared, and a rival compared in no environment leaves the claim. The reading with
  the status quo standing in for every failed plan is printed beside it as a secondary readout. It
  is within the margin where Hall's (1992) one-look reader shows the mean below δ at one-sided 0.025
  (`causaldyn_bench.scorecard.gate`, `Gate(reader="hall")`).
- **δ = 0.05 per euro.**
- **A verdict** is one family at one rung: an intersection-union test (Berger 1982) over the
  comparisons it claims, read at the largest of their levels.
- **A fixed sequence.** The verdicts are tested one at a time at one-sided 0.025, in the declared
  order (`Declaration.order`, ADR 0081): (1, 0), (6, 0), (15, 0), (15, 4), (14, 0). It holds each
  family and rung that claims a comparison and is not exploratory, by its predictive on the sizing
  records (every claimed comparison within together), highest first; ties by family, then rung. A
  verdict holds where it is shown at 0.025 and every verdict before it held. The first verdict that
  is not shown ends the confirmatory claims: every later verdict is read and printed `not
  confirmed: the sequence stopped at family F rung R`. The sequence holds the familywise error at
  0.025 with no adjustment (Maurer, Hothorn and Lehmacher 1995).
- **A comparison is claimed** only where its predictive (Sizing) is at least 0.5. Every other
  comparison is run, read and printed, `not claimed: predicted to fail`. None is (Predicted
  failures).
- **The headline**, "within δ of every maintained MMM", is against each rival at its recommended
  settings, over M5's five verdicts, M4's 26 and M3's six geo verdicts together. It is said only
  where every family and rung is claimed whole, no comparison predicted to fail, and every verdict
  holds: an intersection-union test over the verdicts, each at 0.025, with no adjustment. It cannot
  be said: family 4 at k = 0 is not claimed.
- **The losses.** Each comparison is read the other way too, the rival less the claim's arm
  against 0; Holm's step-down over the claim's comparisons is printed beside the verdicts.
- **The no-test clause.** At k = 0, on every family, the claim's arm less the status quo has a
  paired interval not above 0, read on every family and reported beside the claim.

## Sizing

`m5_sizing.py` beside this file (SHA-256 `9a3fb74b20cf6d5644ac130472498cd78db3e0a7822dfbe64e7547ad700dadb9`), on the sizing records (above):

1. **A comparison's N.** A comparison with n sizing pairs, mean m and SD s (ddof 1), the family's
   environments pooled, has the calibrated predictive at N worlds
   P(N) = Φ((δ − m − z s / √N) / (s √(1/n + 1/N))), z = z(0.975): the normal form of the claim's
   one-look reader, with a flat prior on the true mean that carries the sizing mean's own error. It
   needs the fewest N, in tens, with P(N) at least 0.8: N = ((z² − q²) / (a z − q √(a² + (z² − q²)
   / n)))², a = (δ − m) / s, q = z(0.8), which exists where a > q / √n. A comparison with m ≥ δ is
   not sized: its predictive is at most 0.5 at any N. With an exact sizing mean N is ((z + q) s /
   (δ − m))², and at m = 0 it is M4's tie rule. The closed form, its root, its limit and P(N) = 0.8
   at six points were checked in Maxima.
2. **A rung's N** is the largest over its sized comparisons, at least the floor of 240. **The
   reader's check**: the claim reads with Hall's reader, which corrects for skew, and the normal form
   does not; on a heavy-tailed comparison its N is optimistic (on family 1's tested rung against
   PyMC-Marketing in M4's records, skew −5.4, the normal form gives 0.997 at 240 worlds and the
   bootstrap 0.807). So the rung's N is raised by ten worlds while one of its sized comparisons'
   predictives (rule 3, that comparison alone) is below 0.8. A rung whose N passes the cap of 520
   is exploratory: run, read and printed, never in the fixed sequence, and it does not size its
   family.
3. **The predictive** (`scorecard.development.predictive`), on the sizing records: the two-stage
   bootstrap M3 and M4 ordered their sequences by, 4 000 resamples, seed 20261123.
4. **A family** declares the largest of its claimed rungs' worlds, at least the floor of 240, split
   evenly over its environments and rounded up to whole worlds an environment.
5. **M4's tie rule**, N = (2.8016 · sd / δ)² from the largest SD, is printed beside the rule and not
   taken.

## Predicted failures

## Forecasts

`scorecard.forecast` forecasts no item: M5's forecasts are the verdicts' predictives. The expected
number confirmed is the fixed sequence's: the sum over the order of the running product of the

## Records reused

None. Every rival is fitted on every M5 world; no record of another run is taken in place of a fit,
and no root declares a reuse.

## Reading

As in M4:

- **No interim look.** Every declared world of a family is fitted and scored before its verdicts
  are read.
- **A failed fit** is a failure record after two attempts. It is counted, and it leaves the complete
  cases of every comparison that reads it.
- **A plan off the box or the budget** is moved into the box and counted.
- **A fit past its chains' bounds**: the claim reads the plan as made. A secondary reading gives
  every such plan the status quo's regret.

## Reported regardless

- Every verdict and every comparison's mean, interval, level and word; Holm's losses beside them.
- **Secondary readings, never read by the claim** (`scorecard.claim.secondary_lines`), as in M4.
- Every recorded arm less every rival (`claim read --recorded`).
- Cost: each process's CPU-seconds, wall and peak memory (`logs/costs.jsonl`), and each attempt
  (`logs/attempts.jsonl`).

## Stop rules

- **No stop on an outcome.** Neither a win nor a loss stops a family early.
- **A world's limits.** Each arm runs under its memory cap and timeout (`Declaration.limits`) by
  M4's rule: M2's for the arm's cost class on the family its cost is read from, the timeout
  stretched by the arm's cost factor; family 15's from M2's records of family 15; each family's in
  `m5.json`'s `limits`. Families 1, 6 and 14 take M4's limits. One CPU a process, but Meridian 1.2
  and PyMC-Marketing 1.5. A world stopped twice is a failure record.
- **A defect found during the run**: that arm stops, the fix is committed, and every world of that
  arm is fitted again; the result names it as a deviation.
- **The budget**: $30.30, the worst case of one c6a.48xlarge spot box (Compute), set on 2026-11-23
  on the decision the author delegated, fixed before the run starts. If it runs out, every family
  and rung whose declared worlds are whole is read; the others are reported as not run.

## Compute

one 192-CPU box: a planning figure and not a timing. The same model put M4 at 1 868 core-hours and
19.2 hours for a realised 1 502 and 16.0. Box hours are the makespan times 1.075 for spot stops,
with half an hour to provision and half an hour to pull; prices an hour, spot, $1.22 at the median
and $1.34 at the 90th percentile.

4 800 processes. Core-hours by class: Meridian 182, PyMC-Marketing 138, Orbit 34, `margin` and
`anchored` 1. Family 15's Meridian fits are the critical path: at most 12 Meridian fits run at
once, so the box is about a quarter used. **The box** is a c6a.48xlarge, launched in a zone where
its highest spot price over the last 7 days is at most $1.37 an hour, else not launched.

**The guard**, by M4's rule. The box keeps a clock of its billed hours, its uptime summed over its
boots (`m5-autostop.sh`). After the deadline no world starts whose 90th-percentile wall would end
past it (`M5_DEADLINE_HOURS`); at the hard stop the box powers itself off, once it has also been up
45 minutes since its last boot. Neither reads an outcome. The deadline is the 90th-percentile box
hours plus one, rounded up to a half hour: 11.5 billed hours. The hard stop is three hours past it:
14.5 (52 200 s). The spot bid is at most $1.37 an hour. The worst case is the hard stop plus an hour
at the bid with the address, a 200 GiB gp3 volume for 5 days, the image's snapshot for a month and
30 GB out, as in M4: $30.30, the budget (Stop rules).

If the deadline binds, the roots with the most work are cut first: every root advances in seed
order. A family and rung whose declared worlds are not whole is reported as not run, and the fixed
sequence stops at its first such verdict.
