# The M3 run: pre-registration

**Frozen on 2026-11-17 on the author's confirmation, before any confirmatory world of the run is
fitted.** Every slot is filled. This file, `m3.json` beside it and the sizing script it names,
`m3_sizing.py`, are the M3 run's pre-registration from the commit that marks them frozen. A
change after that commit is a deviation: the result names it and says why.

M2's verdict stands as published and is not read again here. M2's scored worlds are M3's
development set; M3's claim is read on confirmatory worlds no scored run has read.

## Question

On each family of known-truth worlds that M3 covers, and at each of its rungs, does the engine
plan the quarter with a regret per euro of budget no worse than each maintained tool's, and no
worse than the status quo's and the equal split's, by more than a margin δ?

## The development set

- **M2's scored worlds**, every root of M2's run, are the development set: its stored records of
  every rival are read, and no rival is fitted on them again.
- **The split, fixed before any candidate ran** (`scorecard.development.split`). Within each family
  and environment, M2's scored worlds are ranked by their seeds, ascending. A world whose rank
  leaves 2 when divided by 3 is in the `check` split, and so is every world of a `<root>-robyn`
  root. Every other world is in the `fit` split. Candidates are tuned, selected and compared on the
  fit split alone. The check split is read once a candidate rule, for the predictive.
- **The pilot's reading** (`scorecard.development.joined`). A pilot root plans the candidates on
  M2's worlds. Each of its worlds is paired with M2's world of the same family, environment, seed
  and rung, only where the two exports hold the same observation by digest and the built-in arms
  score alike in both.

## Deviations from the pilot's pre-registration

`m3-pilot.md` declared that the claim's arm at each rung is the candidate its selection rule picks
among the pilot's candidates. The pilot ran on 2026-11-16 and 11-17. Its rule picked `dlm cvar 0.1`
at k = 0, `stacked` at the tested rung and `bayes geo plug-in` on the geo families. **The claim's
arms below are not those.** Each is a rule designed after the pilot's first reading, between
2026-11-16 and 2026-11-17. This section says what was done instead, and what it costs the claim.

| Rung | The pilot's winner | The claim's arm | Arm, variant |
|---|---|---|---|
| k = 0 | `dlm cvar 0.1` | the noise mixture with the time guard | `margin`, `margin held` |
| tested rung | `stacked` | `gs held`: `gs curved` with the time guard | composite of `stacked` (`bayes`) and `anchored held` (`anchored`) |
| geo families | `bayes geo plug-in` | `geo eb` | `geo-eb`, `geo eb` |

1. **How each rule was chosen.** Each rule has a dated declaration, written before any of its
   comparisons: the question, the tuning worlds, the freezing rule, the one final read and its bar.
   Tuning read the development set's fit split and worlds of the pilot blocks that no box run fitted
   (families 12 and 13: pilot seeds 30 to 99 of `priors`; family 9: pilot seeds from 30; families 17
   to 20: pilot seeds 30 to 49). The frozen rule was then read once on the check split and on the
   pilot's fresh worlds of families 9, 12 and 13. The selection rule was then run on the development
   set's records of the final arms (laptop stages that ran the claim arms on M2's development worlds
   and the pilot's fresh worlds; no rival refitted): it picks `margin held`, `gs held` and `geo eb`,
   and its 20 verdict predictives equal the research rules' own readings within 0.01. The box
   pilot's own select did not pick them: they did not exist when it ran.
2. **The check split is no longer read once.** `m3-pilot.md` reads the check split once a candidate
   rule. Each rule here was read there once, but the rules were designed in sequence, each knowing
   the readings of the ones before it:
   - k = 0: the pilot's winner, then `margin`, `margin ranged`, `margin curved`, the noise mixture
     (`margin curved noise`) and the noise mixture with the time guard, which equals the noise
     mixture on every world of the check split and of the pilot's fresh worlds;
   - the tested rung: the pilot's winner, then `gs`, `gs curved` and `gs curved` with the time guard
     (`gs held`), whose record is `gs curved`'s bit for bit on every development world;
   - the geo families: the pilot's winner, then `geo eb`.

   The predictives that order the verdicts and name the predicted failures can therefore be
   optimistic.
3. **The optimism at k = 0 was measured before the run.** On 2026-11-17, after the k = 0 rule was
   frozen and before any confirmatory world was drawn, 1 160 worlds were drawn at seeds outside
   every block of the benchmark (700 000 to 714 039; families 0, 2, 3, 5, 7, 1, 4, 6, 14, 12, 13; 40
   an environment), under a declaration written before the draw. The rule's regret less the status
   quo's was read there once and compared with the check split's. Pooled over the scored families
   the check split flattered the rule by **+0.003 (standard error 0.009)**: nothing the audit can
   see. The status quo's own regret, the control, agreed within two standard errors on every family.
   The rule with the time guard was read on the same worlds once: it equals the noise mixture on
   every one of them (no channel is held; the largest correlation of a channel's spend with time is
   0.48). The scored arm's code was then run on the same 1 160 worlds: its regrets equal the
   research rule's to the last bit in 10 of 11 families (1.4e-13 in the other). The scored arm's
   guarded variant `margin held` was run there too: its plan is `margin curved noise`'s bit for bit
   on 1 160 of 1 160 worlds, and it holds no channel. **The geo rule's optimism was measured the
   same way** on 420 fresh world-rungs (seeds 740 000 on; families 8, 10 and 9, 30 worlds an
   environment, both rungs), through the scored arm's code: pooled, the check split flattered the
   rule by +0.005 (standard error 0.006) at k = 0 and +0.007 (0.006) at the tested rung; against
   Meridian, Orbit and the equal split the margins stay below 0 by 0.03 to 0.06; against
   PyMC-Marketing the rule is level (-0.008 to +0.012, standard errors 0.007 to 0.013), inside the
   claim's margin of 0.05. **The tested rung's rule was measured the same way** on 280 fresh worlds
   (seeds 700 000 on; families 0, 2, 3, 5, 7; 20 worlds an environment), the engine's `stacked`
   fitted on the 34 worlds the rule routes to it (no divergence, largest R-hat 1.009; no failed
   record): pooled, the check split flattered `gs curved` by -0.007 (standard error 0.011), that is,
   not at all; the status quo's regret, the control, agreed within 2.0 standard errors on every
   family. The margins predicted from the fresh worlds stay below 0 against Meridian (-0.077,
   standard error 0.010), Orbit (-0.059, 0.011) and the equal split (-0.207, 0.011). Against
   PyMC-Marketing the rule is level: -0.002 pooled (0.011), -0.043 to +0.032 by family (standard
   errors 0.016 to 0.027). Each family's value is inside the claim's margin of 0.05; 40 to 60 fresh
   worlds a family are too few to show that at two standard errors (family 5: +0.032 + 2 x 0.024).
   The read is `gs curved`'s; `gs held` holds no channel on these 280 worlds, so the reading is its
   own. **The scored tree itself was then run on the same audit worlds**: its `margin held` and `gs
   held` give the audited plans and regrets to the last bit on 1 160 of 1 160 and 280 of 280 worlds
   (the same 34 routed; no channel held), and the geo arm's files are byte-equal to the audited
   ones.
4. **What this does not change.** No rule, script or reading above drew a confirmatory world. The
   fixed sequence holds its familywise error for any order fixed before the confirmatory worlds are
   read. δ, the reader, the intersection-union test and the sizing rule are as declared. An
   optimistic predictive costs power, not error: a verdict placed too early that is not shown ends
   the confirmatory claims after it.
5. **One disclosure.** On 2026-11-16 a laptop rehearsal of the run kit exported confirmatory worlds
   270 000 and 270 001 (family 3, `r-0.9`, k = 0) and fitted `ridge` on them. `ridge` is outside the
   claim. No claim arm, rival, research rule or reading read those worlds or records. The two worlds
   stay in the block: nothing that enters a verdict saw them.
6. **The pilot's k = 0 winner runs beside the claim; the geo model's Markov chains do not.** On the
   author's decision of 2026-11-17, `dlm cvar 0.1` runs as a recorded arm (`dlm`) beside `margin
   held` on every fresh world the k = 0 claim's arm plans: the main roots of families 0, 2, 3, 5, 7,
   12 and 13 and of the exploratory families 17 to 20, at each rung of those roots, as a root's arms
   run. It is not run on M2's worlds of families 1, 4, 6, 14 and 15, which the development set read.
   It is neither a rival nor a candidate: `margin held` less `dlm cvar 0.1` is read beside the
   claim, declared before the run (The claim, the pilot's winner). That reading checks, on worlds no
   one has read, that replacing the pilot's winner was right. It costs about 1 322 core-hours, 9 box
   hours and $16 at the median (Compute). `bayes geo plug-in` cost 1 900 to 6 000 CPU-seconds a
   world-rung in M2 (mean 4 520); `geo eb` reads the same exports in 0.1 to 2 CPU-seconds. On the

7. **Where a scored arm departs from its rule as read on the check split.** On every world of the
   parity sets (3 155 worlds at k = 0; the tested rung's and the geo sets) the scored arms' plans

   from the plan with it forced down on 25 of 60 pilot worlds, by up to 7.3 % of the weekly budget).
   The scored arm gives such a channel a margin of exactly 0, which is what the research rule
   computes in exact arithmetic, and holds it at its status quo. It agrees with the research rule on

   channel's adstock. Moving the constant spend by 1e-9 either way moves the scored arm's plan by
   2.8e-10 at most.

## The selection rule

Declared before any M3 pilot ran, in `m3-pilot.md`, the pre-registration the pilot's roots
run under (`scorecard.development.select`):

1. Data: the development set's fit split, M2's stored rival records.
2. For each candidate and each confirmatory family f at rung r: `d(f, r, rival)`, the mean paired
   difference of regret per euro, the candidate less the rival, over the fit split's worlds both
   were read on. A fit that failed plays the status quo, as the bench scores it, and is counted;
   no world is dropped for a failure: a candidate's robustness is part of its quality. The claim
   and the predictive read complete cases (below).
3. A candidate's score at rung r is the largest `d(f, r, rival)` over the confirmatory families
   and rivals.

5. The geo families, 8 and 10 at 3 and 8 geos, are scored apart: the rule runs over those two

6. The winner's predictive (the claim, below) is computed on the check split, never the fit.

## The claim

- **Axis.** Regret per euro of the quarter's budget, as the bench scores it. Lower is better.
- **A comparison** is the claim's arm less one rival, at one rung of one family, paired by world,
  on the complete cases, as in M2: the worlds where both the claim's arm and the rival made a
  plan, each arm's failures counted beside it; an environment where the rival failed on more than
  half of the worlds is not compared, and a rival compared in no environment leaves the claim. The
  reading with the status quo standing in for every failed plan is printed beside it as a secondary
  readout. It is within the margin where Hall's (1992) one-look reader shows the mean below δ at
  one-sided 0.025 (`causaldyn_bench.scorecard.gate`, `Gate(reader="hall")`).
  Its printed word, `beats`, `ties` or `loses to`, is Hall's reader's too.
- **δ = 0.05 per euro.**
- **A verdict** is one family at one rung: an intersection-union test (Berger 1982) over the
  comparisons it claims, read at the largest of their levels.
- **A fixed sequence.** The verdicts are tested one at a time at one-sided 0.025, in the declared
  order (`Declaration.order`, ADR 0081), (family, rung): (0, 0), (0, 4), (2, 0), (2, 4), (5, 0),
  (5, 4), (8, 0), (8, 4), (9, 0), (9, 4), (10, 0), (10, 4), (3, 0), (7, 0), (13, 4), (7, 1), (3, 4).
  It holds each family and rung that claims a comparison and whose N is within the cap, by its
  predictive on the check split (every claimed comparison within together), highest first; ties by
  family, then rung; family 7's tested rung named 1. A verdict holds where it is shown at 0.025 and
  every verdict before it held. The first verdict that is not shown ends the confirmatory claims:
  every later verdict is read and printed `not confirmed: the sequence stopped at family F rung R`.
  The sequence holds the familywise error at 0.025 with no adjustment (Maurer, Hothorn and Lehmacher
  1995). The order names only verdicts the claim reads: a root's declaration refuses an entry of its
  family that is exploratory, at a rung it does not hold, or with every rival predicted to fail or
  capped exploratory (`Declaration.reads`).
- **A comparison is claimed** only where its predictive (sizing, below) is at least 0.5. Every
  other comparison is run, read and printed, `not claimed: predicted to fail`, and listed below
  before the freeze. A family and rung whose every comparison is predicted to fail is printed
  `not claimed`.
- **The tuned rivals are not claimed: no predictive (no development record).** No development
  record of a tuned setting lies on the check split, so `meridian tuned` and `robyn tuned` have
  no predictive and cannot enter the claim. Meridian's tuning chose by its health card and its
  out-of-sample prediction, never by regret, and no reading measured the claim's arm against it.
  Each tuned rival is read and printed beside every verdict of its root, outside the
  intersection-union test, the fixed sequence and Holm's step-down: its root caps it at the
  root's own worlds, the cap exploratory (`Declaration.caps`, `Declaration.exploratory_caps`),
  so it is read on every declared world and printed `on its cap, W worlds an environment` and
  `exploratory`, W the root's worlds an environment. A verdict's predictive, its place in the
  order and the expected count read the recommended rivals alone.
- **The headline**, "within δ of every maintained MMM", is against each rival at its recommended
  settings. It is said only where every family and rung is claimed whole, no comparison predicted
  to fail, and every verdict holds: an intersection-union test over the verdicts, each at 0.025,
  with no adjustment. The tuned columns are reported beside it, never in it.
- **The losses.** Each comparison is read the other way too, the rival less the claim's arm
  against 0; Holm's step-down over the claim's comparisons is printed beside the verdicts.
- **The no-test clause.** At k = 0, on every family, the claim's arm less the status quo has a
  paired interval not above 0, read on every family and reported beside the claim.
- **The pilot's winner beside the claim.** Declared before the run, on the author's decision of
  2026-11-17 (Deviations, item 6): at k = 0, `margin held` less `dlm cvar 0.1`, paired by world on
  the complete cases, each arm's failures counted. It is read run by run, with the paired 95 %
  Student-t interval, on every root that runs both, and pooled over the confirmatory runs at k = 0
  (families 0, 2, 3, 5, 7, 12 and 13): every complete case one world, the variance stratified by
  run, the 95 % interval on the normal quantile (`scorecard.claim.against_default`, declared by
  `Declaration.default_less`; ADR 0094). Families 12 and 13 are past the cap at k = 0 and out of the
  claim, but their worlds are as fresh. The reading is printed beside the claim and enters no
  verdict, no Holm's list and no headline.

## Sizing

`m3_sizing.py` beside this file (SHA-256 `3d1d516bf80413f9ae3673fda4c57320579402b7480992d1e46f30532e30ea8e`), on the development set's records of the
claim's arms (M2's stored records, the records of the claim's arms on M2's worlds, and the
records of families 9, 12 and 13 on their pilot worlds):

1. **N.** For each family and rung, the fewest worlds, in tens, that give 80 % power at a true
   tie: N = (2.8016 · sd / δ)², sd the largest paired SD (ddof 1) of the verdict's comparisons
   with the recommended rivals on the fit split, the family's environments pooled, complete
   cases only. N is sized at 0.025 a verdict: the fixed sequence tests each verdict at 0.025, so
   N carries no inflation for the number of verdicts. A family declares the largest N over its
   rungs, at least the floor of 240, split evenly over its environments and rounded up to whole
   worlds an environment. A family and rung whose N passes the cap of 520 is exploratory: run,
   read and printed, never in the fixed sequence; a family whose largest N passes the cap
   declares the floor.
2. **The predictive** (`scorecard.development.predictive`), on the check split. A bootstrap of two
   stages carries the pilot mean's own error: in each environment, the check split's worlds are
   drawn with replacement, as many as it holds, and from those the declared N; each draw is read as
   the claim reads it, each comparison on its complete cases, a world where either arm failed drawn
   and left out of it, by Hall's reader against δ. A comparison's predictive is
   the share of draws that read it within; a verdict's is the share that read every claimed
   comparison within together, and it sets the verdict's place in the order. 4 000 resamples,
   seed 20261116.
3. **Families 9, 12 and 13**, which M2 never ran, hold no development world: they are read on
   their pilot roots' worlds (`select --fresh`), every one in the check split, since no candidate
   is chosen on them, each for the winner of its group (family 9's geos by the geo winner). Their
   sd and their predictive both come from those worlds.

## Reading

- **No interim look.** Every declared world of a family is fitted and scored before its verdicts
  are read.
- **A failed fit** is a failure record after two attempts. It is counted, and it leaves
  the complete cases of every comparison that reads it.
- **A plan off the box or the budget** is moved into the box and counted.
- **A fit past its chains' bounds** at any stage, the two-stage arm's first stage included, an
  R-hat above 1.01 or divergent draws past their share (`scorecard.report.past`): the claim reads
  the plan as made. A secondary reading gives every such plan the status quo's regret.
