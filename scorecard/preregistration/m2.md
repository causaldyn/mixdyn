# The M2 run: pre-registration

This file, `m2.json` beside it and `m2_sizing.py` become the M2 run's pre-registration when they
are committed with every slot filled and before any scored world of the run is fitted. A change
after that commit is a deviation: the result names it and says why.

## Question

On every family of known-truth worlds that M2 covers, does the engine plan the quarter with a
regret per euro of budget no worse than each maintained tool's, and no worse than the status quo's
and the equal split's, by more than a margin δ?

- **The claim's arm** is `dlm plug-in` at k = 0 and `dlm two-stage` at the tested rung. It is a
  change the pilot selected, declared as such. On the w10 pilot `dlm plug-in` gained less from the
  lift tests than the tools did: its curve search lands on curves a go-dark test cannot reject,
  whose slope in the plan's box is wrong. `dlm two-stage` takes each channel's curve from the static
  Bayesian arm's fit, which reads the tests, and reads the tests again in the DLM, every effect's
  discount 1. Two arms were fixed before an ablation on 37 w10 worlds of families 0, 2, 3, 5, 6 and
  14, at k = 0 and 4, and one pooled rule chose between them
  (`two_stage_ablation.json`, committed before the ablation ran; one probe cell ran first to
  measure memory, a k = 4 record the same in both arms): (a) `dlm two-stage` at every
  rung, mean regret 0.1927; (b) `dlm two-stage` at the tested rung and `dlm plug-in` at k = 0,
  0.1386. The rule picked (b).
- **Every tool** runs its documented setting in its pinned environment (the bench's
  `scripts/*_arm` lock files) and is told the kernel's length, Robyn excepted.
- **Robyn** costs more than 1.5 core-hours a world-rung (1.86 on the w10 pilot), so it plans the
  first 20 scored worlds of each environment at both rungs. The runner plans a capped tool on every
  world of its root, so Robyn runs in a root of its own beside each main root, `<root>-robyn`, with
  `chc`, on those 20 worlds. That root declares Robyn capped at 20 (`Declaration.caps`) with its
  comparisons exploratory (`exploratory_caps`), and the claim reads them as it reads a capped tool
  of the main root (the claim's reading, below).
- **Meridian on family 10** does not run on `geos-50`: one world-rung there took 2.1 CPU-hours and
  16.1 GiB, and three of four were stopped at 3 hours or 20 GiB. On `geos-3` and `geos-8` its
  comparisons are exploratory.
- **The tools' records** are kept from an earlier run only where the export's digest and the
  tool's pinned environment are unchanged. PyMC-Marketing runs again everywhere, on the bench's
  pins.
- **PyMC-Marketing** reads every lift test on its default likelihood, a Normal of the signed
  change. Its pilot at 1.3.0 refused no row in 860 worlds of families 0, 8, 10 and 13 (the bench's
  `results/pymc_marketing_pilot.md`).

## The claim

- **Axis.** Regret per euro of the quarter's budget, as the bench scores it: what the best plan
  returns over the plan's return, on the effect path the best plan is made for. Lower is better.
- **A comparison** is the claim's arm less one rival, at one rung of one family, paired by world.
  It is within the margin where Hall's (1992) one-look reader shows the mean below δ:
  `g(T) >= z_0.975`, with `T = (δ - mean) / (sd / n^(1/2))`, `g(T) = T + a T^2 + a^2 T^3 / 3 + a / 2`
  and `a` the skewness of `δ - difference` over `3 n^(1/2)` (`causaldyn_bench.scorecard.gate`,
  `Gate(reader="hall")`). Hall's transform removes the first-order effect of the differences' skew
  on the one-sided level, which Student's t does not.
- **δ = 0.05 per euro.**
- **The claim** is an intersection–union test (Berger 1982): on a family it holds only where every
  comparison of the family, at both rungs, is within δ. Each comparison is read at one-sided 0.025,
  and so is the claim, with no adjustment. The launch claim holds only where every non-exploratory
  family's claim holds.
- **Complete cases, primary.** A comparison reads the worlds where both the claim's arm and the
  rival made a plan (`scorecard.claim.complete_case`). Each arm's failures are counted beside it.
  An environment where the rival failed on more than half of the worlds is not compared, and a
  rival not compared in any environment leaves the claim. The reading with the status quo standing
  in for every failed plan is printed beside it as a secondary readout. This rule was chosen after
  the w10 numbers of family 7 against Orbit were seen; it is declared as such.
- **Power.** Each family is sized for a joint chance of 0.8 that every one of its comparisons is
  within δ at a true tie. The families' readings are independent, so at a true tie the launch
  claim's chance is the product, 0.8^7 = 0.210, for its 7 non-exploratory families. A launch claim
  that fails at a true tie is the likely outcome, not evidence of a loss: losses are read by
  Holm's step-down.
- **The losses.** Each comparison is read the other way too, the rival less the claim's arm against
  0. Every verdict is printed unadjusted, with Holm's step-down at familywise 0.025 over the claim's
  comparisons beside them.
- **The no-test clause.** At k = 0, on every family, the claim's arm less the status quo has a
  paired interval not above 0. It is read on every family, the exploratory ones too, and reported
  beside the claim.

## Sizing

`m2_sizing.py`, on the w30 pilot's scores, the claim's arm as stated above:

1. Each comparison's paired differences over the pilot's worlds, the status quo standing in for a
   failed plan, as the pilot scored them.
2. Each comparison's differences raised by the larger of two 80 % upper confidence limits of their
   SD, over the SD: the chi-square limit, and the kurtosis-adjusted one, `ln s^2` with standard
   error `((κ - (n - 3)/n) / (n - 1))^(1/2)`. The chi-square limit is too narrow where the
   differences have heavy tails.
3. A bootstrap of the raised differences, centred at 0 and resampled within each environment, the
   same worlds for every comparison (20 000 resamples, seed 1982), each read by Hall's reader.
   `N` is the fewest worlds, in tens, at which every comparison is within δ together with chance at
   least 0.8.
4. The run declares `max(N, 240)` worlds, rounded up to whole worlds an environment. The floor of
   240 keeps Hall's reader near its nominal level under the skew the drift run's differences
   showed. A family whose `N` passes 520 is exploratory and declares 240. A family whose blocks
   hold fewer worlds an environment than it would declare runs on what they hold and is
   exploratory: family 15, on its 100 files.

## Reading

- **No interim look.** Every declared world of a family is fitted and scored before its claim is
  read. A family's claim is read only when it holds its declared worlds whole in every environment.
- **A failed fit** is a failure record after two attempts. It is counted, and it leaves the complete
  cases of every comparison that reads it.
- **A plan off the box or the budget** is moved into the box and counted.
- **A fit past its chains' bounds**, an R-hat above 1.01 or more than 27 of 4 000 draws divergent as
  a share of its draws (`scorecard.report.past`): the claim reads the plan as made, as a user at
  the tool's defaults would ship it. The report gives a second reading with every such plan at the
  status quo's regret.
- **A world where an item's quantity is not scored** is left out of that item, and the reading
  counts it.

## Stop rules

- **No stop on an outcome.** Neither a win nor a loss stops a family early. Losses are published
  beside wins.
- **A world's limits.** Each arm runs under its memory cap and timeout (`Declaration.limits`):
  1.5 times the arm's largest peak memory on the family's pilot, in whole GiB, and 6 times its 90th percentile of wall time a process, each family's in `m2.json`'s `limits`; the most any family's arm gets: `bayes` 2G and 11 580 s; `bayes-geo` 3G and 42 240 s; `chc` 2G and 720 s; `dlm` 11G and 9 060 s; `dlm-two-stage` 3G and 12 120 s; `meridian` 30G and 9 960 s; `orbit` 2G and 660 s; `pymc-marketing` 6G and 12 060 s; `pymc-marketing channel` 3G and 2 160 s; `pymc-marketing shared` 2G and 1 680 s; `ridge` 1G and 300 s; `robyn` 4G and 22 680 s. A world
  stopped twice is a failure record.
- **A defect found during the run**, in an engine arm, a tool's configuration or the harness:
  that arm stops, the fix is committed, and every world of that arm is fitted again. The result
  names the defect, the fix and the worlds it touched as a deviation. Records it replaces are
  kept.
- **The budget.** The run's compute is the author's, fixed before it starts. If it runs out, every
  family whose declared worlds are whole is read; the others are reported as not run. No family is
  read on part of its worlds.

## Reported regardless

- Every comparison's mean, interval and unadjusted verdict, family by family and rung by rung, and
  family 6 by environment; Holm's losses beside them.
- The status-quo-fallback readout beside each complete-case comparison, and each arm's failures by
  family and environment.
- The exploratory families, Robyn's comparisons, Meridian on family 10, and the no-test clause on
  every family.
- Every recorded arm less every rival: `dlm plug-in` at the tested rung, `dlm mixture`,
  `dlm cvar 0.1`, `stacked`, `cvar 0.1`, `ridge`, `chc`, `bayes geo plug-in`
  (`scorecard.claim.recorded`, printed after the claim by `claim read --recorded`).
- `dlm two-stage` less `stacked` at the tested rung (`scorecard.claim.recorded`, the same
  reading). On the ablation it was −0.006 at k = 4 (standard error 0.011): the DLM's second stage
  added almost nothing over the static Bayesian arm.
- The secondary axes (`causaldyn_bench.scorecard.scoring`): regret on the realised path; claimed
  less realised gain and the 90 % gain interval's coverage; the quarter's CRPS at the status quo;
  ROI and mROI coverage; plans moved into the box; fits past their bounds.
- Cost: each arm's CPU time, wall time and peak memory a world-rung.
